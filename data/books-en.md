# Books I Would Recommend to a Friend
**Personal Selection by Marc Duboc (@promaa)**

> *"I put this list together for friends who ask what shaped the way I think, build, and live. These aren’t books to pad an academic CV. They are the texts I keep on my desk—the ones that gave me mental firewalls against noise, taught me patience in difficult projects, or reminded me why craft, solitude, and freedom matter. Take whatever sparks your curiosity, and leave the rest."* — Marc Duboc

---

## 01 / Foundational Pillars & Liberty

### 01. Man’s Search for Meaning
- **Author**: Viktor E. Frankl · 1946
- **Category**: Memoir · Psychology & Ultimate Resilience
- **Personal Note**: Frankl survived Auschwitz and distilled the most liberating psychological truth ever written: you cannot always choose your circumstances, but you always have absolute sovereignty over your attitude. Read this when you feel overwhelmed or adrift; in 150 pages, trivial anxieties evaporate.
- **The Spark**: *Finding a "why" that outlasts any collapse of the "how."*

### 02. The Count of Monte Cristo
- **Author**: Alexandre Dumas · 1844
- **Category**: Classic Novel · Strategy & Long Horizons
- **Personal Note**: The greatest adventure story in world literature, but really a masterclass in composure, patience, and playing the 20-year game. Edmond Dantès never panics; he calculates, builds his leverage, and masters time.
- **The Spark**: *“All human wisdom is contained in these two words: Wait and hope.”*

### 03. Capitalism and Freedom
- **Author**: Milton Friedman · 1962
- **Category**: Essay · Economics & Individual Liberty
- **Personal Note**: Friedman cuts through bureaucratic rhetoric with razor-sharp clarity. Even if you don’t subscribe to every market conclusion, it forces you to question why we surrender so much of our daily autonomy to administrative inertia.
- **The Spark**: *Economic freedom and personal autonomy are fundamentally inseparable.*

### 04. Discourse on Voluntary Servitude
- **Author**: Étienne de La Boétie · 1576
- **Category**: Political Philosophy · 40-Page Essay
- **Personal Note**: Written by an 18-year-old in the 16th century, and still fresher than almost any modern political commentary. His core insight is devastating: tyrants only hold power because the governed consent to obey them.
- **The Spark**: *A short, electrifying shock to the system against passive compliance.*

### 05. On the Liberty of the Ancients compared with that of the Moderns
- **Author**: Benjamin Constant · 1819
- **Category**: Speech / Essay · Political Theory
- **Personal Note**: Constant drew a brilliant distinction two centuries ago: ancient freedom was constant political mobilization; modern freedom is the quiet, sacred right to be left in peace to pursue your craft, family, and ideas without interference.
- **The Spark**: *The foundational case for private sanctuary over permanent political noise.*

## 02 / Systems, Power & Technology

### 06. Manufacturing Consent
- **Author**: Noam Chomsky & Edward S. Herman · 1988
- **Category**: Media Theory · Systems Analysis
- **Personal Note**: Chomsky and Herman break down the 5 structural filters through which mass media selects, frames, and purifies information. It gives you a diagnostic toolkit so you never consume news passively again.
- **The Spark**: *Your personal mental firewall against manufactured consensus.*

### 07. Permanent Record
- **Author**: Edward Snowden · 2019
- **Category**: Memoir · Cybersecurity & Digital Sovereignty
- **Personal Note**: Beyond the NSA disclosures, this is a book about treating technology with ethical seriousness. Snowden reminds us that convenience is a trap: if you don’t maintain your tools, your tools will control you.
- **The Spark**: *Treat your machines, privacy, and permissions with the rigor of a craftsman.*

### 08. Atlas Shrugged
- **Author**: Ayn Rand · 1957
- **Category**: Philosophical Fiction · Individualism & Reason
- **Personal Note**: A monumental philosophical novel defending the primacy of reason, productive individual agency, and the creator’s mind against bureaucratic collectivism and coerced guilt.
- **The Spark**: *The mind is the prime mover of human progress; never sacrifice your agency to unearned guilt.*

### 09. Crépuscule
- **Author**: Juan Branco · 2019
- **Category**: Investigative Pamphlet · French Power Structures
- **Personal Note**: Whatever one thinks of Branco’s polemical tone, the structural breakdown of Parisian oligarchic networks and revolving doors is documented with surgical precision.
- **The Spark**: *A candid look inside the closed circles and institutional machinery of French power.*

### 10. The Clash of Civilizations
- **Author**: Samuel P. Huntington · 1996
- **Category**: Geopolitics · Macro-History
- **Personal Note**: Huntington predicted that post-Cold War conflicts would run along civilizational and cultural fault lines rather than purely ideological ones. An indispensable macro-lens to decode international flashpoints.
- **The Spark**: *A pragmatic compass for navigating global geopolitical fractures.*

### 11. A Liberal Named Jesus
- **Author**: Charles Gave · 2019
- **Category**: Essay · Economics & Parables
- **Personal Note**: An unorthodox, punchy read re-examining the Gospels through the lens of individual agency, capital allocation, and productive risk. It treats responsibility not as a burden, but as a moral duty.
- **The Spark**: *A refreshing perspective on risk-taking, stewardship, and personal accountability.*

## 03 / Solitude, Discipline & Craft

### 12. A Philosophy of Solitude
- **Author**: John Cowper Powys · 1933
- **Category**: Philosophy · Daily Rituals & Stillness
- **Personal Note**: Powys provides practical rituals for cultivating an unshakeable inner fortress: elemental pauses, walking alone, observing wind and stone. Solitude here is not isolation; it is the deliberate cultivation of one's own mind.
- **The Spark**: *The antidote to hyper-connected overstimulation and sensory burnout.*

### 13. The Story of Art
- **Author**: E. H. Gombrich · 1950
- **Category**: Art History · Visual Culture
- **Personal Note**: The antithesis of pretentious art theory. Gombrich writes like an attentive, wise friend strolling with you through an empty gallery, showing you exactly how humanity learned to see light, space, and perspective.
- **The Spark**: *The single best companion before visiting any museum in the world.*

### 14. Vertigo of Dependence
- **Author**: Nathalie Sarthou-Lajus · 2012
- **Category**: Philosophical Essay · Relationships & Vulnerability
- **Personal Note**: A lucid reflection on the illusions of absolute self-sufficiency. Sarthou-Lajus shows that acknowledging our dependencies and vulnerabilities is the true starting point of emotional maturity.
- **The Spark**: *A quiet mirror reminding us that true strength lies in accepting human limits.*

## 04 / Great Narratives & The Human Condition

### 15. Crime and Punishment
- **Author**: Fyodor Dostoevsky · 1866
- **Category**: Psychological Novel · Conscience & Hubris
- **Personal Note**: Dostoevsky takes you inside Raskolnikov’s feverish brain as intellectual arrogance disintegrates against the crushing weight of human conscience. Unbearably tense and utterly profound.
- **The Spark**: *The greatest warning ever written against elevating abstract ideology over human reality.*

### 16. The Brothers Karamazov
- **Author**: Fyodor Dostoevsky · 1880
- **Category**: Classic Novel · Faith, Reason & Passion
- **Personal Note**: Dmitri (passion), Ivan (intellect), Alyosha (faith). Every fundamental conflict of human nature is played out in this family. The "Grand Inquisitor" chapter alone will echo in your head for decades.
- **The Spark**: *The pinnacle of psychological and philosophical literature.*

### 17. The Gambler
- **Author**: Fyodor Dostoevsky · 1867
- **Category**: Novella · Addiction & Psychological Traps
- **Personal Note**: Written in just 26 days to pay off gambling debts, this short novel is a frantic, raw dissection of how obsession and the illusion of control trap even the sharpest minds.
- **The Spark**: *Fast, sharp, and brutally observant of the psychology of compulsive hope.*

### 18. Promise at Dawn
- **Author**: Romain Gary · 1960
- **Category**: Autobiographical Novel · Filial Devotion & Humor
- **Personal Note**: Gary’s tribute to his mother’s boundless, irrational faith in him. It oscillates between laugh-out-loud humor and overwhelming emotional power.
- **The Spark**: *The kind of book that makes you put it down and call your mother immediately.*

### 19. The Life Before Us
- **Author**: Romain Gary (Émile Ajar) · 1975
- **Category**: Novel · Tenderness & Belleville Street Life
- **Personal Note**: Narrated by Momo, an orphaned boy raised by Madame Rosa in Belleville. It has more genuine warmth, human solidarity, and streetwise wisdom in 200 pages than entire literary movements.
- **The Spark**: *A masterclass in humor, tenderness, and finding beauty in the margins.*

### 20. Around the World in Eighty Days
- **Author**: Jules Verne · 1872
- **Category**: Adventure Novel · Stoicism & Ingenuity
- **Personal Note**: I read this before hiking the GR20. Phileas Fogg’s mathematical composure and Passepartout’s relentless loyalty make it the ultimate comfort read on resourcefulness and perseverance.
- **The Spark**: *Pure Victorian rhythm, calm under pressure, and the joy of problem-solving.*

### 21. Small Country
- **Author**: Gaël Faye · 2016
- **Category**: Contemporary Novel · Childhood & Memory
- **Personal Note**: A luminous, heartbreaking story of childhood in Burundi shattered by the onset of civil war. Faye demonstrates how literature, music, and nature serve as the last shields of human dignity.
- **The Spark**: *A poetic testament to memory and resilience in times of upheaval.*
